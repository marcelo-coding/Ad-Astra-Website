About

Our Story

We spent the first years of our decade in Amazon e-commerce buying ads with our own money, so every click hit our margin. Everything we now run for clients comes from that margin math.

Starting on the Seller Side

We have spent ten years in Amazon e-commerce, and the first stretch sat on the seller side, where every advertising decision came straight out of our own margin. The defining project was a product launch that reached $3M in revenue within its first ten months. That shaped how we grow, because growth only counts when the margin survives it.

Portfolio Years

Running other people's accounts raised the stakes, because a portfolio forces every decision to be defensible rather than intuitive. Today we manage advertising for 15 to 20 accounts, and the brands on our desk include Vermont Smoke & Cure, DigDefence Solutions, SWOOC, Oak & Antler Seasoning Company, CO2 Lift, and Giftlot. The results from that portfolio are public on this site. Vermont Smoke & Cure broke $331K in a single month at a 14.34% TACoS and later ran profitably at a 6.60% TACoS with organic sales carrying roughly 78.5% of revenue, while DigDefence Solutions grew its Prime event approximately 76% year over year as its TACoS fell from a 30.79% baseline to the low teens.

Ads as a Director

As an operator led Amazon agency we run Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands, Sponsored Display, Amazon DSP, and AMC as one system. TACoS stays under 15 percent on our flagship accounts because the repetitive daily work runs as code we wrote, versioned, and published. That has been the through line for the decade. The seller years taught us what margin costs, the portfolio years taught us what process scales, and the engineering work turned both lessons into software that never forgets them. To see how that history shows up in an account, read the case studies.

What Drives the Work

Under the titles, we build and we fix. The same instinct that once optimized our own product margins now tunes bid ladders, reporting pipelines, and our weekly calendar. Our week runs on a fixed cadence where search term harvesting, catalog work, target audits, keyword refactoring, and management operations each own a day. That cadence exists because fifteen to twenty accounts stay healthy on rhythm and collapse on improvisation. The tools that grew out of that rhythm are public on the tools page, which lets you read the systems we built to do the thinking with us.
